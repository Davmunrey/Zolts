# 18 — Decision register: questions and default recommendations

## How to use this document

140 decisions. **124 carry a default recommendation: unless you contradict them, they execute as written.** Three of the blocking twelve are now closed (B4, I10, and the design rule in block O); the remaining 🔴 are blocking and only the founder can answer them — they depend on facts (your network, your capital, your risk appetite) that no analysis can substitute.

Usage rule: do not answer everything. Answer the 🔴, strike through the defaults you disagree with, and the rest is settled. An unrecorded decision reopens every six weeks and costs more than a mediocre recorded one.

| 7 | **A5** independent versus acquisition | Redirects where engineering is invested |
| 8 | **A6** the founding "no" | Without it, the first large customer redefines you |
| 9 | **H1** processor versus joint controller | Existential; requires counsel before the first contract |
| 10 | **E8** training on customer data or not | A sales argument or an asset given away; not both |
| 11 | ~~**I10** Act 2 anchor~~ | **Closed: hybrid floor plus lift bonus** |
| 12 | **B7/B8** paid pilot with signed criteria | Determines whether phase 1 feedback is real or polite |
| 13 | **C1** unified CRM API vendor, or native connectors only | Default: buy the long tail once a prospect needs a third CRM. Native stays for the entry segment, because opt-out state is what unified APIs normalise worst. See ADR-013 |
| 14 | **C2** who owns the correctness of a tenant-authored CRM mapping | Default: the tenant, stated in the sync report as a caveat on every run. Revisit if a mapping error reaches a sent message; the alternative is reviewing customer documents, which reintroduces the per-customer cost ADR-014 removes. See ADR-014 |
| 15 | **B9** invitation-only signup, or open self-serve | Default: invitation, minted on the CLI. Revisit when the entry segment is small enough to self-serve at a price that does not justify a sales conversation; until then, control of who gets in is worth more than signup volume. See ADR-015 |
| 16 | ~~**M3** an unclassified reply counts as a conversion~~ | **Closed: it counts, and the measurement says how many.** Deflating it silently would move every tenant's lift on a deploy; a footnote nobody reaches is not a disclosure. The console reports conversions read of conversions total beside the lift, and the share falls on its own as providers send bodies and tenants enable the agent layer. See ADR-016 |
| 17 | ~~**P4** the overage rate per credit~~ | **Closed: `docs/12` already priced it and the runtime had not read it.** Additional credits on the document's own volume ladder (0-100k → €0.010, 100k-500k → €0.008, >500k → €0.006), additional seats at €90/month, both re-read out of `docs/12` by a test. The one real ambiguity — what the ladder is measured over — is resolved **graduated, over the overage alone**, because the alternative makes the invoice fall as consumption rises; the test asserts that property rather than the arithmetic. An enterprise contract overrides both in `negotiated_terms`. See ADR-017 |
| 18 | **P5** what the standard credit ceiling should be above the plan | Default: the plan itself — `tenant.credit_ceiling` is null and nobody overspends by accident. Raising it is provisioning, so the first tenant who wants overage needs an operator. Revisit when a tenant asks twice: a self-service raise is a pricing decision (how far, and whether it needs a card on file) before it is a feature |
| 19 | **P6** whether a per-mailbox capacity guarantee is worth changing sending provider for | Default: no. Smartlead picks the sending mailbox itself, so on that path the gate bounds the volume Zolts releases and the allocated mailbox is an intent, not an observation. Stated in ADR-020 and in the connector rather than implied. Revisit when a customer's compliance review asks which mailbox sent a specific message, or when a provider that exposes the sender is otherwise worth switching to |
| 20 | **P7** who confirms a sending domain's authentication | Default: an operator, via `zolts sending-domain --spf --dkim …`. An unconfirmed domain has zero capacity, so the failure mode is a tenant who cannot send rather than one whose mail is filtered. Revisit when the first partner registers a second domain: a DNS check would remove the human step, and it is a network call in a path that currently makes none |
| 21 | ~~**P8** whether a failed enrichment lookup is billed~~ | **Closed: it is not.** We pay every provider we ask and the customer pays for a field they got. It makes the waterfall's efficiency our margin rather than the customer's problem, which is what ADR-021 and `docs/07` both claim the optimiser is for. Revisit only if measured hit rates in a segment are so low that the absorbed cost exceeds the credit revenue from that segment — which is a signal to drop the segment or the provider, not to change the term |
| 22 | **P9** whether a program may buy a missing field on its own, mid-send | Default: no. `zolts enrich` is a deliberate act by an operator, because buying data silently on every send spends a budget nobody authorised. Revisit when a partner asks: the answer is probably a per-program opt-in with its own ceiling, and the ceiling is the part that needs designing, not the trigger |
| 23 | **S1** who pays for a check that finds nothing | Default: the tenant, because a check is priced per account-day and looking is the service. This is the opposite of the enrichment answer (decision 21) and deliberately so: an enrichment miss means we paid a provider and delivered no field, while a signal check that finds nothing is the surveillance the customer is buying. Revisit if a partner reads the two as inconsistent, which is a pricing-comms problem rather than a pricing one |
| 24 | **S2** whether a stale detection should ever be actionable | Default: never — a detection past its freshness SLA is refused. Revisit only with measured evidence that a late touch on some signal still converts; the count of refused-stale detections per source is recorded precisely so that evidence can exist |
| 25 | **P10** whether a program step blocked by the policy gate is billed | Default: no. A step is charged when the runtime disposed it — sent, generated, or handed to a person — and a step our own compliance engine refused, or one held for budget or capacity, is not. Billing a blocked step would make the safest configuration the most expensive one to run, which is the incentive `docs/11` exists to prevent, and it would put a customer's suppression list on their invoice. Revisit if blocked steps ever become a material share of compute rather than of intent — that is a program to fix, not a line to charge. See ADR-024 |
| 26 | **A2** what makes a stored dossier stale | Default: any signal observed after the one it was written from. It is a comparison rather than a clock, so an account that has done nothing for a month keeps its answer and one that announced a funding round at noon loses it. Revisit if a partner researches accounts that generate signals constantly — there the rule rewrites a 20-credit document weekly and the answer is probably a minimum interval, not a different trigger. See ADR-025 |
| 27 | ~~**P11** what a second sending channel costs~~ | **Closed: it stays a task for a person until a partner asks.** `docs/12` prices one sending action and LinkedIn has no public outbound API; every route to one is a vendor or an automation that breaks a platform's terms, so the price and the vendor are one decision, and the founder has declined to make it in the abstract. A LinkedIn step is created as a human task, and the seam is built so that a partner asking turns it into a definition plus a connector rather than a rewrite. See ADR-027 |
| 28 | **H2** whether an internal action needs the contact's consent | Default: yes. The policy pack requires consent for any channel it does not list, so a CRM task written about a person is denied unless a basis covers it. That is the fail-closed direction and it is deliberate — a task is still processing that person's data. Revisit with counsel if a partner's own CRM write-back is blocked by it, which is the first place it will bite |
| 29 | **A3** whether zero-copy over the customer's warehouse is still the architecture | Default: it is the intent and not the implementation, and the document now says so. ADR-003 was written in the present tense — "Zolts materialises only what execution requires" — and the word *warehouse* appears nowhere in `runtime/`. What ships copies contacts and accounts into Zolts's own Postgres. That is a defensible first architecture and an undefensible sentence: a buyer's security review reads ADR-003 and asks to see the connector.
